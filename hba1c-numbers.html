Understand Your Number

HbA1c of 7, 8 or 9? What Your Number Means and What to Do

You got a number from the lab and maybe one line from your doctor. Here is what it means in plain words, and what you can do about it.

Short answer: HbA1c shows your average blood sugar over about 2 to 3 months.1 An HbA1c of 7% is an average of about 154 mg/dL, 8% is about 183 mg/dL, and 9% is about 212 mg/dL.2 Many adults with diabetes are advised to aim for below 7%, but your doctor may set a different goal for you.1

What HbA1c measures

Sugar in your blood sticks to a protein in your red blood cells. HbA1c is the share of that protein that has sugar stuck to it. The higher your sugar has been, the higher your HbA1c.

It reflects your average over about 2 to 3 months.1 That is why one good week of eating will not change it, and why labs repeat it about every 3 months.

What 7, 8 and 9 mean in daily sugar

HbA1cAverage blood sugar
7%About 154 mg/dL (8.6 mmol/L)2
8%About 183 mg/dL (10.1 mmol/L)2
9%About 212 mg/dL (11.8 mmol/L)2

So every step up of 1 point means your average sugar is roughly 29 mg/dL higher. These are averages. Your sugar may swing above and below them during the day.

The usual cut-offs for adults who are not pregnant are: below 5.7% under the prediabetes range, 5.7% to 6.4% prediabetes, and 6.5% or higher diabetes.3 See borderline diabetes: what to do if you are in the middle band.

What goal should you aim for?

The American Diabetes Association says many non-pregnant adults without severe low-sugar episodes should aim for an HbA1c below 7%.1

  • Lower goal (below 6.5%) can suit people in good health when the treatment carries low risk.1
  • Less strict goals can suit people with frailty, serious other illnesses or limited ability to manage care. For some older adults, a goal of up to 8% is an example.1

Your goal is a decision to make with your doctor. See diabetes in seniors for more on older adults.

How often should you test?

The ADA advises checking at least twice a year. You may need a check about every 3 months if you are not meeting your goal, your treatment has changed, or your health is changing.1

Between lab tests, a home meter shows what is happening day to day. Our tracking system shows how to log it so your doctor can use it.

When HbA1c can mislead

HbA1c is an indirect measure. Anything that changes how fast red blood cells turn over can throw it off. Examples are anaemia, a recent blood transfusion and kidney failure.1 Some haemoglobin variants also interfere with certain lab methods.1

HbA1c also hides swings. Two people can have the same HbA1c, one with steady sugar and one with big highs and lows.1 If your meter readings do not match your HbA1c, tell your doctor. Another test may be needed.

For more on highs and lows, see fasting vs after-meal sugar.

Can you bring it down? What the research shows

Yes, for many people. The most famous test is the UK's DiRECT trial. At 1 year, 68 of 149 people (46%) in the programme group were in remission, meaning HbA1c below 6.5% after stopping diabetes medicine.4

Be careful with how you read that. DiRECT used a strict, supervised programme with a formula diet of about 825 to 853 calories a day for 3 to 5 months, and the doctors stopped diabetes and blood pressure medicine at the start.4 Do not copy that at home. Medicine changes must come from your doctor.

What is practical: eat in a way you can keep, move after meals, sleep enough and track your numbers. See the post-meal walk, what to eat and the 12-week plan.

Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

What does an HbA1c of 7 mean?

It means your average blood sugar over the past 2 to 3 months was about 154 mg/dL (8.6 mmol/L). Many adults with diabetes are advised to aim for below 7%, but your doctor sets the right goal for you.

Is an HbA1c of 8 or 9 dangerous?

An HbA1c of 8 means an average of about 183 mg/dL, and 9 means about 212 mg/dL. Both are above the usual goal and call for a review of your plan with your doctor. Do not wait for the next routine visit.

How long does it take to lower HbA1c?

HbA1c reflects about 2 to 3 months, so it takes around 3 months of steady change to see the full effect. A home meter shows progress sooner.

How often should HbA1c be tested?

At least twice a year. If you are not meeting your goal, or your treatment or health has changed, about every 3 months.

Can HbA1c be wrong?

Yes. Anaemia, a recent blood transfusion, kidney failure and some haemoglobin variants can make it misleading. Tell your doctor if it does not match your meter readings.

Can I stop my medicine if my HbA1c comes down?

Not on your own. As sugar falls, the same dose can become too strong and cause low sugar. Your doctor should lower doses step by step.
