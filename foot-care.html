What actually matters

Diabetes can affect circulation and nerve sensation in your feet over time — a condition called peripheral neuropathy — which means you might not always feel a blister or pressure point forming the way someone else would. Proper, well-fitted, cushioned shoes matter every single time you walk — not sandals, not worn-out footwear.

Check your feet before and after each walk, especially if you've had diabetes for several years or have any nerve involvement. You're looking for redness, blisters, cuts, or unusual pressure marks. It takes thirty seconds.

Worth knowing: Older guidance used to discourage walking for people with nerve damage in their feet, out of concern it would cause ulcers. More recent studies have shown that moderate walking does not increase ulcer risk in most people with neuropathy, provided you're doing the foot checks and wearing proper shoes. This is a safety routine to build alongside walking — not a reason to avoid it.

One more thing: if you take insulin or a sulfonylurea, physical activity can occasionally lower your blood sugar more than expected. If your blood sugar is below 100 before you walk, have a small carbohydrate snack first, and carry something with fast-acting sugar with you.

Before you ask

Should I avoid walking if I have nerve damage in my feet?

Current research doesn't support that older caution. Moderate walking does not appear to increase ulcer risk in most people with neuropathy, as long as you're checking your feet and wearing proper shoes.

What counts as "proper" shoes?

Well-fitted, cushioned, closed footwear — not sandals, and not shoes that are worn out or no longer supportive.
