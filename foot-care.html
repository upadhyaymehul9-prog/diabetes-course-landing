The Routine

Your 30-second foot check

Build this around every walk — before you go, and after you're back.

Wear proper, well-fitted, cushioned shoes every single time — not sandals, not worn-out footwear.

Before you walk, look over both feet for anything already there — redness, blisters, cuts, pressure marks.

After you walk, check again, especially if you've had diabetes for several years or have any nerve involvement.

If you're on insulin or a sulfonylurea and your blood sugar is below 100 before you walk, have a small carbohydrate snack first and carry something with fast-acting sugar.

What Actually Changed

Older caution vs. current research

Older guidance

Discouraged walking for people with nerve damage (peripheral neuropathy) in their feet, out of concern it would cause ulcers.

Current research

Moderate walking does not increase ulcer risk in most people with neuropathy, provided you're doing the foot checks and wearing proper shoes.

This is a safety routine to build alongside walking — not a reason to avoid it. Diabetes can affect circulation and nerve sensation in your feet over time, which means you might not always feel a blister or pressure point forming the way someone else would. That's exactly why the check matters more than the caution.

Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

Should I avoid walking if I have nerve damage in my feet?

Current research doesn't support that older caution. Moderate walking does not appear to increase ulcer risk in most people with neuropathy, as long as you're checking your feet and wearing proper shoes.

What counts as "proper" shoes?

Well-fitted, cushioned, closed footwear — not sandals, and not shoes that are worn out or no longer supportive.
