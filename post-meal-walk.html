The 10-Minute Walk

Walking After Meals for Blood Sugar: How Long, How Soon, How Fast

Most articles stop at "go for a walk." This page gives the numbers from the studies, a routine you can start today, and a simple way to test it on your own sugar readings.

By Dr. Mehul Upadhyay · Last updated 15 November 2026 · 6 minute read

Short answer: Walk for 10 to 15 minutes at an easy pace, starting within 30 minutes of finishing your meal. If you can do it only once a day, do it after dinner. In one trial this lowered the after-meal sugar rise by about 12% across the day and about 22% after the evening meal (Reynolds et al., 2016).

Why a walk after eating lowers blood sugar

After a meal, the carbohydrate you ate enters your blood as glucose. In most people sugar peaks somewhere between 30 and 90 minutes after eating. In type 2 diabetes and prediabetes, the body responds poorly to insulin, so that peak is higher and lasts longer.

Working muscle has a second way to take in glucose. When your leg muscles contract, they pull glucose out of the blood without needing insulin to do it (Colberg et al., 2016). That is why the timing matters. A walk taken while sugar is rising uses that glucose as it arrives. The same walk taken four hours later misses the peak.

This also explains why a slow walk is enough. You do not need to get fit for this to work. You only need the big muscles of your legs to keep moving while the meal is being absorbed.

What the studies found

Three pieces of research answer most practical questions. They are small, so read them as good guidance and not as a guarantee for every person.

StudyWhoWhat was comparedResult
Reynolds et al. (2016), Diabetologia41 adults with type 2 diabetes, average age 6010 minutes after each main meal, against 30 minutes once a day at any time. Two weeks each.After-meal sugar rise about 12% lower with meal-timed walks. About 22% lower after the evening meal.
DiPietro et al. (2013), Diabetes Care10 inactive adults aged 60 or more with raised fasting sugar15 minutes after each meal, against one 45-minute walk in the morning or afternoon.Both improved 24-hour sugar. Only the after-meal walks clearly lowered sugar in the 3 hours after dinner.
Buffey et al. (2022), Sports MedicineReview of 7 trials in adults, mostly overweightSitting all day, against short standing breaks, against short light-walking breaks.Light walking lowered after-meal glucose and insulin. Standing helped a little. Walking was better than standing.

What these studies do not show

  • They measured sugar over hours or weeks. They did not measure HbA1c over months, weight loss, or remission.
  • The groups were small: 41 people, 10 people, and seven short lab trials.
  • A walk does not cancel a heavy meal. It trims the peak. What and how much you eat still decides most of the rise. See does food order matter and the plate model.

The American Diabetes Association position statement on exercise also advises adults with type 2 diabetes to break up long sitting every 30 minutes with light activity, and to reach at least 150 minutes of activity a week (Colberg et al., 2016). Three 10-minute walks a day comes to 210 minutes a week.

How long, how soon, how fast

QuestionAnswer
How long?10 to 15 minutes. Even 2 to 5 minutes is better than sitting.
How soon?As soon as is comfortable. Within 30 minutes of the last bite.
How fast?Easy pace. You can talk in full sentences.
Which meal?Dinner first. Then the meal with the most rice, roti or sweets.
Where?Anywhere. Terrace, corridor, society compound, or inside the house.

A 3-week start

  1. Week 1: one walk a day, after dinner, 10 minutes.
  2. Week 2: add a second walk after lunch. At work, walk the corridor or the stairs.
  3. Week 3: add a third walk after breakfast. Raise any walk to 15 minutes if it feels easy.

Adding one walk a week is slower than starting all three on day one. It is also the version most people are still doing in month three.

If you cannot go outside

  • Walk inside the house or march on the spot for 10 minutes.
  • Stand and do slow calf raises while holding a chair.
  • Wash the dishes, fold clothes or tidy up on your feet. The aim is to avoid sitting or lying down straight after the meal.

Is 100 steps after dinner enough?

Many Indian families already have the habit of a hundred steps after the night meal. A hundred steps takes about one minute. It is better than sitting, but the studies above used 10 to 15 minutes. Keep the habit and stretch it.

Test it on your own sugar in 6 days

A study tells you what happened on average to other people. Your glucometer tells you what happens to you. This small test needs six evenings and six test strips.

  1. Pick a dinner you eat often. Eat the same dinner, in the same amount, on all six evenings.
  2. Evenings 1 to 3: sit as usual after dinner. Check your sugar 2 hours after the first bite. Write it down.
  3. Evenings 4 to 6: walk for 10 to 15 minutes, starting within 30 minutes of finishing. Check your sugar 2 hours after the first bite. Write it down.
  4. Take the average of the first three readings and the average of the last three. Compare.

One reading can swing for many reasons, so use the average of three. Do not change your medicines based on this test. Show the numbers to your doctor.

Who must be careful

If you take insulin or a sulfonylurea (glimepiride, gliclazide, glipizide, glibenclamide): a walk can push your sugar too low. Carry 15 grams of fast sugar, such as glucose tablets or half a glass of fruit juice. Know the signs: shaking, sweating, sudden hunger, confusion. Ask your doctor before you start. More on this in low blood sugar and what to do.

If you have heart disease (past heart attack, heart failure, angina, a stent): get your doctor's clearance first. Digestion already raises the work of the heart. Stop and seek care if you get chest pain, breathlessness or dizziness.

  • Numb feet or neuropathy: wear closed, well-fitting shoes and check your feet after each walk. See foot care before you start walking.
  • Poor balance or a recent fall: walk indoors near a wall or rail, or use the standing options above.
  • Acid reflux or a very heavy meal: keep the pace gentle and wait 10 to 15 minutes before you start.
  • On metformin alone: a low from a short walk is uncommon. Do not change your dose yourself. See on metformin, read this.
Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

How long should I walk after eating to lower blood sugar?

Ten to fifteen minutes is the range used in the main studies. In a trial of 41 adults with type 2 diabetes, a 10-minute walk after each main meal lowered the after-meal sugar rise by about 12% compared with one 30-minute walk a day (Reynolds et al., 2016).

How soon after a meal should I start walking?

Start soon. In the Reynolds trial people started within 5 minutes of finishing the meal. Blood sugar usually peaks 30 to 90 minutes after eating, so a walk that starts within about 30 minutes still covers the rise.

Is a slow walk enough, or do I need to walk fast?

A slow, relaxed walk counts. A review of seven studies found that even light walking breaks lowered after-meal glucose and insulin compared with sitting (Buffey et al., 2022). You should be able to talk in full sentences.

Which meal matters most if I can only walk once a day?

Dinner. In the Reynolds trial the benefit was largest after the evening meal, about 22% lower, when people ate the most carbohydrate and sat the most afterwards.

Can walking after meals cause low blood sugar?

It can if you take insulin or a sulfonylurea such as glimepiride or gliclazide. Carry 15 grams of fast sugar, know the signs of a low, and ask your doctor before you start. With metformin alone, a low from a short walk is uncommon.

Is 100 steps after dinner enough?

A hundred steps takes about one minute. It is better than sitting, but the studies that showed a clear drop used about 10 to 15 minutes. Treat 100 steps as the start, not the target.

Is walking after meals safe if I have heart disease?

Talk to your doctor first if you have had a heart attack, heart failure, angina or a stent. Digestion already raises the work of the heart, and exercise adds to it. Stop and seek care if you get chest pain, breathlessness or dizziness.
