The Two Numbers, Explained

Where you actually land on each scale

Fasting glucose

Measured first thing in the morning, before eating or drinking anything except water.
<100 Normal100–125 Prediabetic126+ Diabetic

Post-meal (postprandial) glucose

Checked roughly two hours after you start eating.
<140 Normal140–199 Impaired200+ Diabetic

Think of it this way: HbA1c is like your bank's quarterly statement. Fasting and post-meal glucose are like checking your account balance day to day. You need both views to understand the full picture — if you only check fasting and it looks fine, you could be missing dramatic post-meal spikes entirely.

Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

Which number matters more?

Neither on its own gives the full picture — that's the point. A normal fasting number doesn't rule out post-meal spikes, and checking only after meals misses how your body manages glucose overnight.

How often should I actually check these?

This course's tracking module covers exact frequency and logging, so the pattern becomes visible over time rather than a one-off number you can't interpret.

Why does this matter beyond just knowing the numbers?

Both numbers trace back to the same underlying mechanism — insulin resistance. Understanding where you stand is what makes the nutrition, movement, and tracking modules in this course make sense as a connected system, not separate tips.
