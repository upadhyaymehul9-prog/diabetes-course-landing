Common myths we hear constantly

  • "Karela (bitter gourd) juice every morning will cure your diabetes"
  • "If you eat sweets even once, your sugar is ruined forever"
  • "Diabetes means you can never eat rice or mangoes again"
  • "Walking 10 minutes after any meal fixes everything"
  • "Ayurvedic or herbal medicine can replace your prescribed treatment"

What's actually true, and what isn't

Karela juice
May have a mild, short-term effect on blood sugar for some people, but it is not a treatment and won't replace medication or lifestyle change.
One sweet "ruins" your sugar
Myth. A single food doesn't undo weeks of good management — but consistent daily patterns do matter.
Total rice/mango ban
Myth, usually. Portion and timing matter far more than total elimination for most people — this course covers exactly how.
Post-meal walking
True, and genuinely useful — a short walk after eating does measurably help blood sugar. One of the few myths that holds up.

Why myths spread so easily

Diabetes management is genuinely confusing, and a 10-minute doctor's visit rarely has time to explain the "why" behind any instruction. That gap gets filled with WhatsApp forwards and well-meaning advice from relatives — some of it harmless, some of it actively counterproductive.

The evidence standard this course uses: Every claim here is checked against published clinical research, including trials like the UK's DiRECT study on structured lifestyle change — not family folklore, not social media trends.

Before you ask

Should I stop all home remedies?

Not necessarily — some are harmless and culturally meaningful. The issue is relying on them instead of evidence-based management, not using them at all. Always check with your doctor if you're combining remedies with prescribed treatment.

Is this course going to tell me rice and sweets are totally fine?

No — it'll tell you the honest, specific truth about portion, timing, and frequency, instead of a blanket "never" or "always."
