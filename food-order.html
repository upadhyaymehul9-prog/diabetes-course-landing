Same Plate, Same Quantity — Different Sequence

How you eat matters almost as much as what you eat

Eating vegetables and protein before rice or roti produces a smaller blood sugar spike than eating them mixed together, or eating carbs first — layered onto the same simple plate model used throughout this course: half vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter grain.

1

Vegetables & salad first

Non-starchy vegetables and salad go first, lining the stomach before anything else arrives.

2

Dal & protein next

Protein slows digestion further and builds fullness before any carbohydrate shows up.

3

Roti or rice last

The same grain, the same portion — arriving last instead of first tends to raise blood sugar more gently.

Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

Does this mean I should eat less rice or roti?

No. This is about the order you eat your existing plate, not the quantity. The portion guidance is covered separately in the nutrition protocol if you want to work on that too.

Does this actually work with a real Indian thali?

Yes — the course applies the plate model and the sequencing technique directly to North Indian, South Indian and Gujarati meals, not a generic Western plate.

How do I know if it's actually working for me?

Check your post-meal glucose reading about two hours after a meal, using the tracking system covered in the course, and compare it to a similar meal eaten in a different order.
