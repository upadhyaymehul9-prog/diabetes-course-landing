Borderline Diabetes: Sugar Level Chart, Symptoms and What To Do

Your doctor said "keep an eye on it." This page explains what the numbers mean, how likely it is to become diabetes, and what the trials say you can do about it.

By Dr. Mehul Upadhyay · Last updated 26 November 2026 · 7 minute read

Short answer: Prediabetes means a fasting sugar of 100 to 125 mg/dL, a 2-hour sugar of 140 to 199 mg/dL, or an HbA1c of 5.7% to 6.4%. It does not always become diabetes. In a large American trial, losing 7% of body weight and walking 150 minutes a week cut the risk by 58% (Knowler et al., 2002). In an Indian trial, lifestyle change cut it by 28.5% (Ramachandran et al., 2006).

What "borderline" means in numbers

"Borderline diabetes", "borderline sugar" and "prediabetes" mean the same thing. Your blood sugar is above normal, but not high enough to be called diabetes. Any one of these three tests can show it.

TestNormalPrediabetesDiabetes
Fasting sugar (8 hours without food)Below 100 mg/dL100 to 125 mg/dL126 mg/dL or more
2-hour sugar after 75 g glucose drink (OGTT)Below 140 mg/dL140 to 199 mg/dL200 mg/dL or more
HbA1c (3-month average)Below 5.7%5.7% to 6.4%6.5% or more

These are the American Diabetes Association cut-offs (American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee, 2025a). Some labs and doctors use the WHO fasting range of 110 to 125 mg/dL for prediabetes, so a fasting value of 100 to 109 may be reported as normal on one report and borderline on another.

Three things to know about these numbers

  • Risk rises along the range. An HbA1c of 6.3% carries more risk than 5.7%. The cut-offs are lines drawn on a slope, not a switch.
  • One test is not the full picture. Fasting sugar can be normal while the 2-hour value is high. If your result is close to a line, ask your doctor about repeating it or adding a second test.
  • HbA1c can mislead in some people. Anaemia and some haemoglobin conditions change the result. See understanding HbA1c and fasting vs after-meal sugar.

How common is it in India?

Very common. The ICMR-INDIAB study surveyed 113,043 adults across India. It estimated that 15.3% of adults have prediabetes. That is about 136 million people, more than the 101 million who already have diabetes (Anjana et al., 2023).

Prediabetes usually has no symptoms. Most people find it on a routine health check, a pre-employment test, or a report done for some other reason. That is why "I feel fine" is not a reason to ignore it.

Will it turn into diabetes?

Not always. But without any change, the risk is real. Two large trials followed people with prediabetes for two and a half to three years.

TrialWhoWith no programmeWith lifestyle change
Diabetes Prevention Program, USA (Knowler et al., 2002)3,234 adults, average age 51, average BMI 34About 11 in 100 developed diabetes each year58% lower risk. Metformin alone gave 31% lower risk.
Indian Diabetes Prevention Programme (Ramachandran et al., 2006)Indian adults with impaired glucose tolerance55% developed diabetes in 3 years39.3% developed diabetes. That is 28.5% lower risk.

How to read these results

  • The Indian trial enrolled people whose 2-hour sugar was already high. That is a higher-risk group than someone with only a slightly raised fasting value. Do not read "55%" as your personal risk.
  • Lifestyle change lowered the risk in both countries. It did not bring it to zero. Some people in the lifestyle groups still developed diabetes.
  • The American programme was intensive, with regular coaching. Results from a plan you run alone may be smaller.

The honest summary: prediabetes is the stage where your own actions change the outcome the most.

What to do now

The American trial gave its lifestyle group two targets (Knowler et al., 2002). They are still the best-tested starting point.

TargetWhat it means for you
Lose 7% of body weightAt 80 kg, that is about 5.6 kg. At 70 kg, about 5 kg. Not a crash diet. A steady loss over months.
150 minutes of activity a week30 minutes of brisk walking, 5 days a week. Three 10-minute walks after meals also add up. See walking after meals.

Food changes that were used in the Indian trial

The Indian programme did not use a special diet. It advised people to (Ramachandran et al., 2006):

  • eat less in total
  • cut down refined carbohydrates such as white rice, maida and sweets
  • cut down fat and fried food
  • avoid added sugar
  • eat more fibre-rich foods such as vegetables, whole pulses and whole grains

For a practical way to do this on an Indian plate, see the plate model diet chart and the Indian diet page.

Your first 12 weeks

  1. Week 0: write down your weight, waist, fasting sugar and HbA1c. This is your baseline.
  2. Weeks 1 to 2: stop sugary drinks and sweets on normal days. Start a 10-minute walk after dinner.
  3. Weeks 3 to 6: change the plate. Half vegetables and salad, a quarter dal, curd, paneer, egg or other protein, a quarter roti or rice.
  4. Weeks 7 to 12: build up to 150 minutes of walking a week. Add two short strength sessions if you can.
  5. Week 12: weigh again. Repeat fasting sugar. Ask your doctor when to repeat HbA1c.

When to see your doctor sooner

Do not wait for a yearly check if: your fasting sugar is 126 mg/dL or more, your HbA1c is 6.5% or more, or you have strong thirst, frequent urination, unplanned weight loss or blurred vision. These need a proper diagnosis, not a lifestyle plan alone.

  • Retesting: people with prediabetes are advised to be checked for diabetes at least once a year (American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee, 2025b).
  • Medicine: metformin lowered the risk in both trials above. Some doctors prescribe it for higher-risk people. This is your doctor's decision. Lifestyle change is advised either way.
  • Planning a pregnancy, or had diabetes in a past pregnancy: tell your doctor. The targets and timing are different.
  • Also get checked: blood pressure and cholesterol. They often rise together with sugar. See diabetes and blood pressure.
Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

Is borderline diabetes the same as prediabetes?

Yes. Both mean blood sugar above normal but below the diabetes range: fasting 100 to 125 mg/dL, 2-hour sugar 140 to 199 mg/dL, or HbA1c 5.7% to 6.4%.

Will prediabetes definitely become diabetes?

No. Without any change the risk is high, about 11 in 100 people a year in the American Diabetes Prevention Program. Lifestyle change cut that risk by 58% in that trial and by 28.5% in the Indian Diabetes Prevention Programme.

Can prediabetes go back to normal?

In many people, yes. Sugar levels can return to the normal range with weight loss, food changes and regular activity. The change has to be kept up, and yearly testing should continue.

My HbA1c is 5.8%. Should I worry?

It is at the low end of the prediabetes range. It is not an emergency, and it is not something to ignore. Treat it as an early signal, start the changes on this page, and repeat the test when your doctor advises.

How much weight do I need to lose?

The tested target is 7% of your body weight. At 80 kg that is about 5.6 kg. In the American trial, people in the lifestyle group reached about this much in the first year.

Do I need metformin for prediabetes?

Not everyone does. Metformin lowered the risk by 31% in the American trial and by 26.4% in the Indian trial. Doctors may suggest it for higher-risk people. Do not start or stop it on your own.

How often should I test my sugar if I am prediabetic?

At least once a year, or more often if your doctor advises. Test sooner if you get strong thirst, frequent urination, unplanned weight loss or blurred vision.

What is a borderline sugar level?

A borderline sugar level is above normal but below the diabetes range: fasting 100 to 125 mg/dL, 2-hour sugar 140 to 199 mg/dL, or HbA1c 5.7% to 6.4% (American Diabetes Association cut-offs). Some labs use a fasting range of 110 to 125 mg/dL.

What are the symptoms of borderline diabetes?

Usually there are none. Most people find out from a routine health check or a report done for another reason, so feeling fine is not a reason to skip testing. If you get strong thirst, frequent urination, unplanned weight loss or blurred vision, see your doctor sooner.
