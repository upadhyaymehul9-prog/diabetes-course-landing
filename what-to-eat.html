What to Eat With Type 2 Diabetes: Foods That Help and Foods to Limit

A simple guide by food group. For a plate and sample day, see our diet chart. For rice and roti, see our Indian diet page.

Short answer: the American Diabetes Association asks you to eat more non-starchy vegetables, whole fruits, legumes, lean proteins, whole grains, nuts and seeds, and less sugary drinks, sweets, refined grains and ultra-processed foods.1 Aim for at least 14 g of fibre per 1,000 calories.1 There is no single "diabetes diet." Meal plans should be built around you.1

Eat more of these

  • Non-starchy vegetables: leafy greens, lauki, tinda, bhindi, cabbage, cauliflower, beans, tomatoes.
  • Legumes: dal, rajma, chana, sprouts.
  • Whole grains: whole wheat, jowar, bajra, ragi, oats.
  • Lean protein: curd, paneer, eggs, fish, chicken.
  • Nuts and seeds: a small handful.

This follows the ADA's advice to emphasise these groups and choose high-fibre, minimally processed carbohydrates.1 The ADA also says some adults may benefit from reducing carbohydrate, mainly by cutting processed foods.1

Dal and legumes: what the trials show

A systematic review of 41 randomised trials found that eating pulses alone, such as chickpeas, beans, peas and lentils, lowered fasting blood glucose and fasting insulin in people with and without diabetes.2 Pulses are a source of slowly digested carbohydrate, fibre and plant protein.2

That supports dal, rajma and chana as everyday foods. Watch the oil and salt you cook them in. See the vegetarian diet guide for protein ideas.

Fruit: whole is better than juice

In three large US cohort studies, eating more whole fruit, especially blueberries, grapes and apples, was linked with a lower risk of type 2 diabetes. Fruit juice was linked with a higher risk.3 These studies cannot prove cause, and they looked at risk of getting diabetes, not at how people with diabetes should eat.3

The ADA lists whole fruit among the foods to emphasise.1 Keep portions modest and eat fruit with meals. See fruit and diabetes.

Eat less of these

  • Sugary drinks and sweets. See sugar, jaggery or honey?
  • Refined grains: maida, white bread, bakery items.
  • Ultra-processed and packaged foods. The ADA advises minimising them.1
  • Red meat. The ADA advises minimising it.1
  • Foods high in saturated fat. The ADA advises limiting them.1

ICMR-NIN's Dietary Guidelines for Indians also say to avoid added-sugar biscuits and cakes, fruit juices and cold drinks.4

What to drink

Water is the base. Tea or coffee without sugar is fine for most people. See chai, coffee and sweeteners. If you drink alcohol, the ADA advises staying within daily limits and knowing that sugar can fall hours later.1 See alcohol and diabetes.

Turning this into a plan

  1. Ask for a dietitian. The ADA advises referral for everyone with diabetes or prediabetes.1
  2. Use the plate method. See an Indian diet chart.

Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

What foods should I eat with type 2 diabetes?

Non-starchy vegetables, whole fruits, legumes, lean proteins, whole grains, nuts and seeds, according to the American Diabetes Association.

What should I avoid?

The ADA advises minimising sugary drinks, sweets, refined grains, ultra-processed foods and red meat, and limiting foods high in saturated fat.

Is dal good for diabetes?

Trials of pulses such as lentils, chickpeas and beans found they lowered fasting glucose and insulin. Cook them with little oil.

Is fruit juice bad for diabetes?

In large cohort studies, fruit juice was linked with a higher risk of type 2 diabetes, while whole fruits such as blueberries, grapes and apples were linked with lower risk. Whole fruit is the better choice.

How much fibre do I need?

The ADA advises at least 14 g of fibre for every 1,000 calories.

Is there one best diet for diabetes?

No. The ADA recommends individual meal plans based on food quality, total calories and your goals.
