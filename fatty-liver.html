Fatty Liver and Type 2 Diabetes: Why Weight Loss Helps Both

Fatty liver is very common with diabetes and often has no symptoms. Here is what it is, how to check for scarring, and what helps.

Short answer: fatty liver, now called MASLD, affects around 70% of people with type 2 diabetes.1 Most people have no symptoms.2 Diabetes Canada advises considering a simple blood-based check for liver scarring, the FIB-4 score, in everyone with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes.1 Losing at least 5 to 10% of body weight is recommended, and more than 10% is preferred.1

What fatty liver is

MASLD stands for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease. It was previously called fatty liver or NAFLD. It means fat has built up in the liver in someone who has at least one metabolic risk factor, such as obesity, type 2 diabetes, abnormal cholesterol, high blood pressure or metabolic syndrome.2

Most people with MASLD have no symptoms.2 That is why it is often found by accident on a scan.

How common is it with diabetes?

Diabetes Canada says MASLD affects around 70% of people with type 2 diabetes, compared with about 22% of people with type 1 diabetes.1 Many people with diabetes have this condition without a formal diagnosis.1

Fat in the liver is linked to fat around the belly. For Asian Indians, a consensus statement describes fat deposited in organs such as the liver and muscle as a feature of obesity in this group.3 See belly fat and type 2 diabetes and PCOS and insulin resistance.

The scarring check that matters

Fat alone is the early stage. The real danger is scarring (fibrosis). Diabetes Canada calls liver fibrosis the main driver of liver and non-liver problems in people with MASLD.1

Its guideline suggests considering a screening test called FIB-4 in everyone with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes. It is worked out from a few routine blood results and your age.1

FIB-4 scoreWhat it suggests
Below 1.3Low risk. Manage diabetes and weight in primary care.4
1.3 to 2.67Additional tests may be needed.4
Above 2.67High risk of advanced scarring. Referral to a liver specialist is warranted.1

Ask your doctor whether you need this check. Do not interpret the number alone.

What helps: weight loss first

Diabetes Canada says sustained weight loss of at least 5 to 10% of body weight is recommended. Losing more than 10% is preferred, because that can help reverse liver scarring.1 Its patient summary says losing at least 5 to 10% can reduce fat in the liver, and a 10% loss may even help reverse some scarring.4

  • For a 80 kg person, 5% is 4 kg and 10% is 8 kg. See weight loss with diabetes.
  • Eat more vegetables, legumes and whole grains. See an Indian diet chart.
  • Move daily. See the post-meal walk.
  • Be careful with alcohol. See alcohol and diabetes.
  • Avoid unproven "liver detox" products. See home remedies: do they work?

Questions to ask your doctor

These questions are our suggestion.

  • Do I need a FIB-4 check or a liver scan?
  • How much weight should I aim to lose, and over how long?
  • Could any of my diabetes medicines help or harm my liver?
  • Is any alcohol safe for me?
  • When should we repeat the tests?

Diabetes medicines and the liver

Some diabetes medicines may reduce liver fat and slow liver damage.4 That is a conversation to have with your doctor, who knows your full health picture. Never change medicines yourself. See why not to adjust medicine yourself.

Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

What is MASLD?

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, the new name for fatty liver or NAFLD. It means fat in the liver in a person with at least one metabolic risk factor such as type 2 diabetes or obesity.

How common is fatty liver in type 2 diabetes?

Around 70% of people with type 2 diabetes have it, according to Diabetes Canada, and many have never been diagnosed.

Does fatty liver cause symptoms?

Usually not. Most people feel fine, which is why it is often found by chance.

What is a FIB-4 score?

A simple score from a blood test and your age that helps estimate the risk of liver scarring. A score above 2.67 suggests referral to a liver specialist.

Can fatty liver be reversed?

Losing at least 5 to 10% of body weight can reduce liver fat, and a loss of 10% or more may help reverse some scarring, according to Diabetes Canada.

Do I need to stop alcohol?

Alcohol can harm the liver further, so ask your doctor how much, if any, is safe for you.
