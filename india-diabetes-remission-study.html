Indian Evidence

Can Indians Reverse Type 2 Diabetes? What a 2,384-Person Indian Study Found

Most remission research comes from Europe. Here is a large 2025 study of Indian adults, in plain words, including what it cannot prove.

Short answer: in a 2025 study of 2,384 Indian adults with type 2 diabetes, 31.2% reached remission after one year of an intensive online lifestyle programme.1 That means about 3 in 10, not everyone. Remission here meant an HbA1c below 6.5% for at least three months, off diabetes medicine.1 The study had no control group and has real limits, so treat it as encouraging, not as a promise.

Why this study matters for India

Many famous remission studies were done in the UK and other Western countries. Indians often develop type 2 diabetes at a lower body weight and at a younger age. So people ask: does remission work for us too?

This 2025 study, published in the journal PLOS ONE, is one of the larger Indian answers so far. The researchers looked at the records of adults who took part in a one-year online lifestyle programme at the Freedom from Diabetes Clinic in India.1

For a plain explanation of the word itself, read is diabetes reversal real?

What the people did

The study included 2,384 adults aged 30 to 75 with type 2 diabetes and a BMI of 23 or more. They joined a paid, one-year online programme between May 2021 and August 2023.1

The programme combined four things:1

  • A plant-based diet with calorie targets in phases.
  • Exercise planned for each person.
  • Online psychological support.
  • A doctor adjusting medicines step by step, guided by the person's own daily sugar readings.

The programme also used supervised fasting phases. Please do not copy that part on your own. If you take insulin or tablets that can cause low sugar, fasting without medical guidance can be dangerous. Read low blood sugar first.

At the start, the typical person was 51 years old, had lived with diabetes for 7.6 years, had a BMI of 27 and an HbA1c of 7.5%. About 88% were already taking diabetes tablets.1

What they found

ResultWhat the study reported
Reached remission744 of 2,384 people (31.2%)1
How remission was definedHbA1c below 6.5% for at least 3 months, without glucose-lowering medicine1
Weight changeAbout 8.5% lower in the remission group, compared with about 5.2% in the others1
HbA1c changeAbout 15.3% lower in the remission group, compared with about 12.4% in the others1
Insulin resistanceFasting insulin and HOMA-IR (a measure of insulin resistance) improved more in the remission group1

Put simply: about 7 in 10 people did not reach remission in this programme. Many still improved. But remission was not the usual result.

Who did best

The researchers looked for factors linked with remission. People were more likely to reach it if they:1

  • were 50 years or younger,
  • had a BMI of 25 or more at the start,
  • had not yet started diabetes medicine (drug-naive),
  • had diabetes for 6 years or less,
  • had a baseline HbA1c below 7%,
  • lost more than 10% of their body weight.

The common thread is simple: earlier is better. A shorter time with diabetes and a lower starting HbA1c went with better chances. If you are newly diagnosed, our guides for the newly diagnosed and for young-onset diabetes are a good place to start.

What this study cannot prove

Read this part carefully. The authors themselves list the limits. A fair reader should keep them in mind.
  • No control group. The study did not compare these people with a similar group who did nothing. So it cannot prove the programme caused the remission.1
  • It looked backward. It was a retrospective analysis of past records, not a planned trial.1
  • Selection bias is possible. The programme was a paid subscription, and people with incomplete data were left out. People who stay in a paid programme may differ from the rest.1
  • Medicine changes varied. Doctors tapered medicines in different ways.1
  • Digital skills. Participants were likely more comfortable with online tools than the average patient.1

The authors declared no competing interests. Still, the study analysed the programme run by the clinic where the lead author works, so it is worth reading with that in mind.

Remission also is not a cure. Sugar can rise again, especially if weight returns. See long-term maintenance and realistic results.

What you can do with this

  1. Take the encouraging part seriously. In a large Indian group, a meaningful share reached normal numbers without medicine. That is a real signal.
  2. Act early. The factors linked with success favour a short time with diabetes and a lower starting HbA1c. Do not wait.
  3. Aim for steady weight loss if you carry extra weight. See weight loss with diabetes and belly fat.
  4. Never stop medicine on your own. In the study, a doctor adjusted medicines step by step using daily readings. That is the safe model. Talk to your doctor first. See managing without medicine.
  5. Track your numbers so your doctor can act on them. See tracking your numbers.
Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

What percentage of Indians reached diabetes remission in this study?

In a 2025 study of 2,384 Indian adults with type 2 diabetes in a one-year intensive online lifestyle programme, 31.2% (744 people) reached remission. Remission was defined as an HbA1c below 6.5% for at least three months without glucose-lowering medicine.

Does this mean 31% of all Indians with diabetes can reverse it?

No. These were people who joined and stayed in a paid, intensive programme, and the study had no control group. The result may not apply to everyone with diabetes.

Who is most likely to reach remission?

In this study, people aged 50 or younger, with a BMI of 25 or more, no previous diabetes medicine, diabetes for 6 years or less, a starting HbA1c below 7%, and weight loss of more than 10% did better.

Can I stop my diabetes tablets after losing weight?

Do not stop on your own. In the study, a doctor reduced medicines step by step, guided by daily sugar readings. Ask your doctor how to do this safely.

Is remission the same as a cure?

No. Remission means your numbers are normal without medicine for a period of time. Sugar can rise again, especially if weight returns, so you need to keep monitoring.

Was the study reliable?

It was large and Indian, which is useful. But it was retrospective, had no control group, and may have selection bias. It is encouraging evidence, not proof.
