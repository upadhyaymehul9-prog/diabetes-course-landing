What's actually happening

As you lose weight and your insulin sensitivity genuinely improves, your body needs less external help managing glucose. The same dose that was appropriate — even necessary — three months ago can become too strong for your improved body.

This matters most with insulin and sulfonylurea medications, because they directly lower your blood sugar. Metformin alone rarely causes hypoglycemia on its own — it doesn't force your body to release insulin the way a sulfonylurea does. But on insulin or a sulfonylurea, the exact same dose that was safe at week one can push you into genuinely risky territory by week eight.

The real thresholds (ADA guidelines): Below 70 mg/dL is hypoglycemia. 54–69 is Level 1 — an alert threshold needing fast-acting carbohydrates. Below 54 is Level 2 — clinically significant, can impair thinking and coordination. Level 3 means you need someone else's help to recover.

Before you ask

If my numbers are improving, shouldn't I need less medication?

Possibly — but that determination has to be made by your doctor, looking at your full trend data, your HbA1c, and your specific medication. Not by you, based on how you feel.

What should I actually bring to my doctor appointment?

Your tracker covering at least the past four weeks, whether your fasting and post-meal numbers are trending down, flat, or up, any recent HbA1c results, and a written list of your current medications and doses exactly as you're taking them.

Is this caution specific to this course, or standard medical advice?

It's standard, serious medical guidance — not something specific to this program. Self-adjusting glucose-lowering medication while your sensitivity is changing is one of the more common, entirely preventable reasons people on structured lifestyle programs end up needing urgent care.
