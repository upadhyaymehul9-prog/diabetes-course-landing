The plate model

Half the plate
Non-starchy vegetables — leafy greens, cucumber, tomato, cauliflower, bottle gourd, okra. Anything except potato or corn.
A quarter of the plate
Lean protein — dal, paneer, eggs, fish, chicken, or whichever source fits your regular diet.
The final quarter
A complex carbohydrate — a portion of roti, rice, or another grain.
Alongside it
A small amount of healthy fat — roughly a teaspoon of ghee or oil, used in cooking, not piled on separately.

This isn't a diet brand and it's not a rigid meal plan you follow exactly every day. It's a structure you apply to almost any meal you already eat — including your own regional cuisine.

Before you ask

Do I need to weigh my food to follow this?

No. You don't need to weigh or calculate anything — just notice the rough proportions on your plate and adjust gradually. Small, repeated adjustments beat one perfect plate you never repeat.

Does this work for vegetarians?

Completely. The plate model needs protein, not meat specifically — dal, paneer, curd, tofu, chickpeas, and rajma all work. Swap the protein source, keep the same proportions.

Does the order I eat food in actually matter?

Research on meal sequencing generally shows eating vegetables and protein before carbohydrates produces a smaller blood sugar spike than eating everything together, or carbs first. It's a free adjustment — same food, different order.
