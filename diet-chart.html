Diabetes Reversal Diet Plan: An Indian Diet Chart Using the Plate Method

No weighing, no calorie maths. Build each plate from the same simple groups, using foods you already eat.

Short answer: fill most of your plate with non-starchy vegetables, add protein (dal, paneer, curd, eggs, fish, chicken), and keep whole grains or millets to a measured share. The American Diabetes Association advises more vegetables, whole fruit, legumes, lean protein, whole grains, nuts and seeds, and less sugary drinks, sweets, refined grains and ultra-processed food.1 Get personal advice from a registered dietitian.1

What the experts advise

The ADA's 2026 nutrition advice is not a single diet. It says to build individual meal plans around food quality, total calories and your goals.1 In general it asks you to:1

  • Emphasise non-starchy vegetables, whole fruits, legumes, lean proteins, whole grains, nuts and seeds.
  • Minimise red meat, sugary drinks, sweets, refined grains and ultra-processed foods.
  • Choose high-fibre carbohydrates, at least 14 g of fibre for every 1,000 calories.
  • Consider cutting carbohydrate for some adults, mainly by limiting processed foods.

India's national guidelines point the same way. ICMR-NIN's Dietary Guidelines for Indians advise a varied diet with pulses, nuts, milk and eggs or fish, and say to avoid high-fat, high-sugar, high-salt foods and added-sugar items such as biscuits, cakes, fruit juices and cold drinks.2

Build your plate

This is our simple way to apply that advice, not an official chart. Look at your plate at lunch and dinner:

Part of the plateWhat goes there
HalfNon-starchy vegetables: palak, lauki, tinda, bhindi, cabbage, cauliflower, beans, salad.
A quarterProtein: dal, rajma, chana, sprouts, paneer, curd, egg, fish or chicken.
A quarterWhole grain or millet: 1 to 2 rotis of whole wheat, jowar or bajra, or a small portion of rice.

Eat vegetables and protein first, then the grain. See food order for why this helps after-meal sugar. For rice versus roti, read rice, roti or millets.

A sample day

This is an illustration of the plate idea, not a prescription. We give no calorie target, because that depends on your weight, activity and medicines. Ask your dietitian to set one.

MealExample
Early morningWater, or unsweetened tea or coffee. See chai, coffee and sweeteners.
BreakfastBesan chilla or moong dal cheela with vegetables and curd. Or vegetable upma or poha with extra vegetables and a boiled egg or sprouts.
Mid-morningA small portion of whole fruit, or a handful of nuts. See fruit and diabetes.
LunchLarge salad, dal or kadhi-style protein, sabzi, 1 to 2 rotis (whole wheat, jowar or bajra) and curd.
EveningRoasted chana, sprouts, or buttermilk. Avoid biscuits and fried snacks.
DinnerVegetable soup or sabzi, paneer, fish, chicken or dal, and a small grain portion. Eat earlier if you can, and walk afterwards. See the post-meal walk.

Easy swaps

  • White rice → smaller portion, or brown rice, or millets, with more dal and vegetables.
  • Maida roti, naan or paratha → whole-wheat, jowar or bajra roti.
  • Fruit juice or cold drink → water, nimbu pani without sugar, or buttermilk.
  • Biscuits or namkeen → roasted chana, nuts or sprouts.
  • Sweets → keep them for rare occasions, in small portions. See sugar, jaggery or honey? and festival season.

Watch out for these traps

  • "Sugar-free" packaged foods. The ICMR-NIN guidelines warn that they can still contain fat, refined flour and hidden sugars, so read the label.3
  • Too little protein. If you are losing weight, the ADA advises making sure you still get enough protein and micronutrients.1
  • Skipping meals if you take insulin or a sulfonylurea. This can cause low sugar. See low blood sugar.
  • "Cure" diets and powders. Be cautious of any single-food or supplement claim. See common myths.

Make it yours

The ADA advises everyone with prediabetes or diabetes to be referred to a registered dietitian.1 Ask for one. Then check what works for your body with a meter, comparing your sugar 2 hours after meals. See tracking your numbers and fasting vs after-meal sugar.

Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

What is a good diet chart for diabetes in India?

Build each plate from half non-starchy vegetables, a quarter protein such as dal, paneer, curd, eggs or fish, and a quarter whole grain or millet. This matches the ADA's advice on food quality, adapted to Indian foods.

Can I eat rice and roti with diabetes?

Yes, in measured portions, with vegetables and protein first. Whole grains and millets are better choices than refined ones.

How much fibre should I aim for?

The ADA advises at least 14 g of fibre for every 1,000 calories, from minimally processed carbohydrates.

Are sugar-free products safe for diabetes?

Not automatically. The ICMR-NIN guidelines warn that some sugar-free foods contain fat, refined flour and hidden sugars. Read the label.

Is this diet plan enough to reverse diabetes?

Diet is one part. Remission studies also involved weight loss, activity and medical supervision. See our page on whether reversal is real.

Do I need a dietitian?

The ADA advises referral to a registered dietitian for everyone with prediabetes or diabetes. They can set calories and portions for you.
