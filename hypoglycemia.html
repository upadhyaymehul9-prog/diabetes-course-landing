Low Blood Sugar (Hypoglycemia): Signs, the 15-15 Rule and What to Do

Low sugar can happen fast. Know the numbers, the treatment and the warning signs before you need them.

By Dr. Mehul Upadhyay · Last updated 27 November 2026

Short answer: low blood sugar is a reading below 70 mg/dL.1 If you are awake and can swallow, use the 15-15 rule: take 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrate, then recheck in 15 minutes and repeat until your sugar recovers.2,3 If a person is confused, cannot swallow or has passed out, do not give food or drink. Call for emergency help and use glucagon if it is available.3,6

The three levels of low sugar

LevelWhat it means
Level 1Sugar below 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) but 54 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L) or higher1
Level 2Sugar below 54 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L)1
Level 3A severe event with confusion or changed physical state that needs another person's help, whatever the reading1

Common warning signs include hunger, sweating and chills.5 Level 3 involves an altered mental state.5 Signs differ from person to person, so a reading is the safest way to know. If you feel odd, check your sugar.

How to treat a low: the 15-15 rule

  1. Check your sugar if you can. If you cannot, treat anyway when you have symptoms.
  2. Take 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrate. The CDC lists hard candies or jellybeans, 3 to 4 glucose tablets, or one dose of glucose gel.3 The ADA's guide also lists 1 tablespoon of sugar or half a cup of juice or non-diet soft drink.6
  3. Wait 15 minutes and check again. If you are still below 70 mg/dL, repeat.3
  4. Repeat until you recover, then, if your next meal is more than an hour away, have a snack with 15 g of carbohydrate and a protein source.4

Keep glucose tablets or sugar with you, in your bag and by the bed. If you often need treatment, tell your doctor. Do not wait for your next visit.

Severe lows: when it is an emergency

If a person is unconscious, having a seizure, or cannot swallow safely, do not put food or drink in their mouth. Call emergency services. If glucagon is available and you know how to use it, give it. Glucagon is usually followed by waking within about 15 minutes, and the person still needs medical care.3,6

The ADA's guide says glucagon should be prescribed for people at risk of low sugar.6 Diabetes Canada adds that family or friends should be taught how to give it.4 If you take insulin, ask your doctor about this.

Who is at risk?

  • People on insulin or sulfonylurea tablets. Metformin alone rarely causes low sugar, but it can when added to these medicines or heavy alcohol. See metformin and why not to adjust medicine.7
  • People who drink alcohol. Sugar can drop hours later. The ADA advises counselling on delayed low sugar after drinking, and on checking glucose afterwards.8 See alcohol and diabetes.
  • People who skip meals or fast. See fasting (vrat) with diabetes and intermittent fasting.
  • People who are losing weight. The same dose can become too strong. See weight loss with diabetes.
  • Others on the ADA risk list: recent low-sugar episodes, kidney disease, dementia, or not feeling the warning signs.2

How to prevent lows

  • Review your plan after a serious low. The ADA says one or more level 2 or 3 episodes should prompt a review of your treatment.1 The ADA's guide says a level 3 low is a reason to ease back on treatment and re-teach the person about diabetes.6
  • Consider a continuous glucose monitor. The ADA recommends one for people who use insulin or who have had, or are at high risk of, low sugar.2
  • Do not change doses yourself. See managing without medicine.
  • Track patterns. Note when lows happen. See tracking your numbers and our free tracker.
  • Tell the people around you what to do. See for family members.
Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

What blood sugar level is too low?

Below 70 mg/dL is low blood sugar. Below 54 mg/dL is more serious. A severe low is any low that leaves you needing help from another person.

What is the 15-15 rule?

Take 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrate, wait 15 minutes, then check your sugar again. If it is still low, repeat.

What should I eat for low blood sugar?

Fast-acting sugar, such as glucose tablets or gel, hard candies, 1 tablespoon of sugar, or half a cup of juice or non-diet soft drink.

When is low sugar an emergency?

If the person is confused, cannot swallow, has a seizure or is unconscious. Do not give food or drink. Call emergency services and use glucagon if available.

Can metformin cause low blood sugar?

Rarely on its own. Low sugar is more likely when it is combined with insulin, a sulfonylurea or a lot of alcohol.

What if I have lows often?

Tell your doctor soon. Your treatment may need to be changed, and a glucose monitor may help.
