Why This, Not A Generic Plan

A plan built for someone with decades ahead, not decades behind

Longer time since diagnosis

The DiRECT trial found people were less likely to reach remission the longer it had been since their diagnosis — a window that closes gradually over time.

Your window: diagnosed young

Being diagnosed young, with decades ahead, puts you closer to that shorter-time-since-diagnosis window — making early, structured action one of the highest-leverage decisions you can make.

Dr. Mehul Upadhyay has spent 15+ years working inside hospital operations and healthcare quality management — including time in the New Zealand healthcare system, where earlier-onset Type 2 diabetes is increasingly common and treated as a priority for early, structured intervention. This course brings that same urgency and structure to a younger diagnosis. Always discuss your specific case with your doctor — remission is not guaranteed for anyone.

Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

Is Type 2 diabetes different when you're diagnosed young?

The underlying condition works the same way, but the stakes and opportunity are different — more years of potential complications if unmanaged, but also more years for structured change to compound in your favor. Always discuss your specific case with your doctor.

Will this work around an active social life?

Yes. The framework is built around real decisions at real social occasions, not a plan that only works if you stay home every night.

Is remission more likely at a younger age?

The evidence suggests a shorter time since diagnosis is linked to better odds, which often correlates with younger age at diagnosis — but it's not guaranteed for anyone. Always talk to your doctor about your individual case.
