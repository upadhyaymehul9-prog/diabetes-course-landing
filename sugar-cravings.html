What's Actually Happening

The spike-and-crash cycle behind the craving

1

A meal spikes your glucose

A carb-heavy meal without enough protein or fibre sends blood sugar up sharply.

2

The crash follows

Your body overcorrects, and glucose drops sharply a few hours later.

3

The craving hits

Shakiness, irritability, or fogginess appear — and they read as a craving for something sweet.

Rapid drops in blood glucose after a spike trigger hormonal and neurological hunger signals that strongly resemble cravings — which is why meal structures that reduce glucose variability consistently reduce reported cravings in people with insulin resistance.
Breaking the Cycle

What actually breaks the craving cycle

Drives the Crash

A carb-heavy snack eaten alone

Without protein or fibre to slow absorption, a quick sugary fix sends glucose up fast — setting up the next crash and craving.

Breaks the Cycle

The same craving, paired with protein or fibre

A realistic food swap that satisfies the craving without sending blood sugar back up sharply, so the cycle doesn't repeat itself.

Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

Will this mean giving up sugar completely?

No. The focus is on meal structure and timing that prevents the spike-and-crash cycle — not permanent, all-or-nothing restriction, which usually backfires anyway.

What if my cravings are at night, not after meals?

Night-time cravings are often linked to how your last meal of the day was structured, plus sleep quality — both of which this course addresses directly.

Is this a replacement for my doctor?

No. This is structure and knowledge to have better conversations with your doctor — never a replacement for medical advice.
