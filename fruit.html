Common Indian Fruits

Not all fruit behaves the same way

Fruit isn't one category — fiber content, ripeness, and portion size change how fast it raises blood sugar. Here's a general picture; the course covers your specific plan in detail.

Watch Portion

Mango, Banana, Grapes

Higher natural sugar density — not off-limits, but portion and timing (after a meal, not on an empty stomach) matter more here than with other fruits.

Generally Easier

Guava, Apple, Papaya, Pear

Higher fiber-to-sugar ratio tends to produce a gentler blood sugar response for most people, skin-on where possible.

Avoid This Form

Fruit Juice (Any Fruit)

Juicing removes the fiber that slows sugar absorption — a glass of juice affects blood sugar very differently than the same fruit eaten whole.

What Actually Matters

Three rules that matter more than any "banned fruit" list

What people assume

"Certain fruits are simply forbidden, full stop — the fruit itself is the problem."

What actually matters

Portion size, whether you eat it alone or with protein/fat, and timing relative to your other meals — these move the needle far more than which fruit it is.

Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to give up mango completely?

For most people, no — it's about portion and timing, not elimination. Your doctor knows your specific case best, especially if you have other health conditions.

Is dried fruit the same as fresh fruit?

No — drying concentrates the sugar into a much smaller portion size, so the same "handful" of dried fruit affects blood sugar more than the fresh equivalent.

Is this covered in the course, or do I need a separate diet plan?

It's part of the nutrition module's plate-model approach — fruit is covered alongside everything else you eat, not treated as a special, separate topic.
