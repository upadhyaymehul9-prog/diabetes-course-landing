Indian Diet

Indian Diet for Diabetes Reversal: Can You Keep Roti, Rice and Dal?

You do not have to give up Indian food. You do need to watch how much white rice you eat and what goes with it.

Short answer: yes, you can keep your roti, rice and dal, but white rice is the one to watch. A meta-analysis of 7 prospective cohort analyses found that Asians who ate the most white rice had a 55% higher risk of type 2 diabetes than those who ate the least.1 It was an observational study, so it shows a link, not proof.1 Choose whole grains, add dal and vegetables, and keep portions measured.2

What the research says about white rice

A 2012 meta-analysis in the BMJ pooled prospective studies of white rice and type 2 diabetes. In Asian populations, the risk was 55% higher when comparing the highest rice intake with the lowest. In Western populations, who eat far less rice, the 12% rise was not statistically significant.1 Asian participants ate an average of 3 to 4 servings a day, against 1 to 2 servings a week in the West.1

These were observational studies, so other habits may play a part. The practical lesson is to avoid very large portions of white rice, not to ban it. Read rice, roti or millets for the full comparison.

Dal, rajma and chana belong on your plate

A systematic review of 41 trials found that pulses lowered fasting glucose and insulin in people with and without diabetes.3 The ICMR-NIN guidelines also advise a variety of pulses, nuts, and milk, eggs or fish for protein and nutrients.4

Make dal a main part of the meal, not a thin side. Go easy on oil and salt. See what to eat by food group.

Roti and millets

The ADA asks people with diabetes to prefer whole grains and minimise refined grains.2 So a whole-wheat, jowar, bajra or ragi roti is a better choice than a maida roti, naan or bakery bread. It also helps you reach the ADA's fibre target of at least 14 g per 1,000 calories.2

Count your rotis. For most meals, 1 to 2 is a sensible start, but ask your dietitian for your number.

Regional ideas

  • North Indian: dal, mixed sabzi, salad and 1 to 2 whole-wheat rotis. Ask for less oil in the gravy and skip naan.
  • South Indian: sambar with vegetables, a smaller portion of rice, and thoran or poriyal. Watch idli and dosa portions and add protein.
  • Gujarati: dal and kadhi with sabzi and bajra or jowar rotla. Watch sugar in dal and kadhi.
  • Bengali and others: fish with vegetables and a small portion of rice.

These are example swaps, not tested menus. For vegetarian ideas see the vegetarian guide.

Watch out for these

  • Large rice portions at festivals and weddings. See festival season.
  • Added-sugar items: ICMR-NIN advises avoiding added-sugar biscuits and cakes, fruit juices and cold drinks.4
  • Fasting days. If you take insulin or a sulfonylurea, skipping food can cause low sugar. See fasting (vrat) with diabetes.

Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

Can I eat rice if I have diabetes?

In measured portions, with vegetables and protein, yes. A meta-analysis found a higher diabetes risk with very high white rice intake in Asian populations, so avoid large portions of white rice.

Is roti better than rice?

Whole-grain rotis made from wheat, jowar, bajra or ragi are better choices than refined flour or large portions of white rice. Portion size still matters.

Is dal good for diabetes?

Yes. Trials found pulses lowered fasting glucose and insulin. Cook dal with little oil and salt.

How many rotis can I eat?

It depends on your weight, activity and medicines. One to two per meal is a common starting point. Ask a dietitian to set your number.

Do I have to give up Indian food?

No. Adjust portions, choose whole grains, add dal and vegetables, and limit sweets and packaged foods.
