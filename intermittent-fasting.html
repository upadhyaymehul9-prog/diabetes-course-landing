The Honest Answer

It depends entirely on your medication

Not your willpower, not your discipline — your medication. Here's the split that actually matters.

On Insulin or a Sulfonylurea

Fasting windows carry real risk

These medications lower your blood sugar directly. A fasting window removes the food that would normally buffer that effect, which can push you into dangerous low blood sugar (hypoglycemia). This isn't a decision to make on your own — it needs your doctor, who knows exactly which medication and dose you're on.

Not on Glucose-Lowering Medication

Structured fasting may be an option

If you're not on insulin or a sulfonylurea and your doctor clears it, some people do find structured fasting windows helpful. But it is never a default recommendation in this course — the risk profile depends entirely on your individual treatment, and getting it wrong isn't a minor mistake.

Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

Does this course teach intermittent fasting as its main method?

No, and we want to be upfront about that. The course's core method is the structured plate model, walking, and tracking system — not a fasting protocol. Intermittent fasting is addressed honestly as a safety question for people considering it, not taught as the program.

Why not just teach a fasting protocol — isn't it popular?

Because the risk depends entirely on individual medication, and getting that wrong can mean a genuine medical emergency. A structured, non-fasting approach is something nearly everyone can start safely, which is why it's the foundation here.

I'm not on any medication — can I still take the course?

Yes. The course works independent of whether or not you fast, and it will never ask you to adopt a fasting protocol as part of the 12 weeks.
