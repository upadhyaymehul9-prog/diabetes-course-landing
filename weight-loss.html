Weight Loss With Type 2 Diabetes: What Works and How Much You Need

Losing weight can improve sugar a lot, but it is harder than it sounds. Here is how much helps, and how to do it safely.

By Dr. Mehul Upadhyay · Last updated 27 November 2026

Short answer: the American Diabetes Association advises at least 5 to 7% weight loss for people with overweight or obesity.1 About 5% is the minimum for metabolic benefit, and benefits rise up to around 15%.1 For a 70 kg person, 5% is 3.5 kg. Larger loss can lower HbA1c and fasting sugar substantially and may lead to remission.2

How much weight do you need to lose?

The ADA says people with overweight or obesity should have a treatment plan that aims for at least 5 to 7% weight loss.1 Its text describes about 5% as the minimum for health benefits and says more loss brings more benefit, up to about 15%.1

In people with type 2 diabetes who are overweight, modest weight loss improves blood sugar and reduces the need for glucose-lowering medicine. Larger weight loss substantially lowers HbA1c and fasting sugar and may lead to sustained remission.2

What about BMI for Indians?

A WHO expert consultation found that Asian populations often carry more body fat and more diabetes risk at a lower BMI. It identified 23.0 and 27.5 kg/m² as public-health action points for Asians, along with the standard WHO cut-offs.3

If your BMI is lower, you can still have type 2 diabetes, and belly fat matters too. See belly fat and diabetes and fatty liver.

What the research shows

DiRECT (UK). In a trial using a supervised formula diet of about 825 to 853 calories a day for 3 to 5 months, then slow food return and support, 46% of the programme group (68 of 149) were in remission at 1 year.4 That programme also stopped diabetes and blood pressure medicine at the start. Do not try it alone.

India (2025). In a one-year online lifestyle programme for 2,384 Indian adults, 31.2% reached remission. People who reached it lost about 8.5% of body weight on average, against about 5.2% in those who did not. Losing more than 10% and having a BMI of 25 or more at the start were linked with remission.5 The study had no control group, so it shows a link, not proof.

Lose weight safely on diabetes medicine

Check with your doctor first. If you take insulin or a sulfonylurea, eating less can drop your sugar too low. Do not skip meals or fast without medical advice.
  • Know the low-sugar signs. See low blood sugar.
  • Do not change your own doses as you lose weight. See why not to adjust medicine yourself.
  • Protect your muscle and nutrition. The ADA advises regular monitoring of people who are losing weight on purpose, to make sure they get enough protein and micronutrients.1

What works in daily life

  1. Eat in a way you can keep. The ADA stresses individual meal plans and a dietitian referral.1 See an Indian diet chart and what to eat.
  2. Walk after meals. See the post-meal walk and movement.
  3. Sleep and stress. Poor sleep and stress push cravings up. See stress, sleep and blood sugar and sugar cravings.
  4. Plan for keeping weight off. Regain is the main reason remission fades. See long-term maintenance.
  5. Ask about medicines that help weight. See GLP-1 medicines vs lifestyle.

Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

How much weight should I lose with type 2 diabetes?

The ADA advises at least 5 to 7% of your body weight if you have overweight or obesity. About 5% is the minimum for benefit, and more loss, up to about 15%, brings more benefit.

Can losing weight reverse type 2 diabetes?

For some people it can lead to remission. In the Indian study, people who reached remission lost about 8.5% of weight on average, and losing more than 10% was linked with it. It is not guaranteed.

Is the Asian BMI cut-off different?

A WHO expert consultation identified 23.0 and 27.5 kg/m2 as public-health action points for Asian populations, because risk appears at lower BMI.

Is it safe to crash diet with diabetes?

No. If you take insulin or a sulfonylurea, eating very little can cause dangerous low sugar. Plan weight loss with your doctor.

Why does weight come back?

In the DiRECT trial, remission fell as weight returned. Staying at a lower weight needs a plan you can live with and long-term support.

Should I tell my doctor before I start?

Yes. Your doctor may need to lower your medicine as you lose weight, and can advise a safe pace.
