The Mechanism, Not Just The Symptoms

PCOS and insulin resistance feed each other

PCOS

A large share of women with PCOS have measurable insulin resistance — it's a core part of the condition for many, not a separate issue.

Insulin resistance

PCOS itself raises long-term risk of Type 2 diabetes, which is why improving insulin sensitivity matters for both conditions together.

That's why improving insulin sensitivity through structured lifestyle change is a recommended approach for both conditions together — and why this course targets the insulin resistance underneath, not just calories in and out. Dr. Mehul Upadhyay has spent 15+ years working inside hospital operations and healthcare quality management — including time in the New Zealand healthcare system — and applies that same structured, evidence-based thinking here rather than treating PCOS and diabetes risk as unrelated problems.

Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

Is this course specifically for PCOS?

No — it's a structured insulin-resistance and weight-loss program built around Type 2 diabetes. Many of the same mechanisms apply to PCOS-related insulin resistance, but it isn't a PCOS-specific medical treatment plan. Talk to your gynecologist or endocrinologist about your specific diagnosis.

Will this fix my PCOS symptoms?

This course doesn't diagnose or treat PCOS directly — it's educational content on insulin resistance and structured weight loss. Many PCOS symptoms are linked to insulin resistance, so improving that can help, but always manage PCOS itself with your doctor.

Is this a replacement for my doctor?

No. This is structure and knowledge to have better conversations with your doctor — never a replacement for medical advice.
