Is Diabetes Reversal Real? What Remission Means and What the Research Shows

Many programmes promise a cure. Here is what experts actually call it, what the best studies found, and what they could not promise.

Short answer: for some people with type 2 diabetes, remission is real. A cure is not. Experts define remission as an HbA1c below 6.5% at least 3 months after stopping diabetes medicine.1 In the UK DiRECT trial, 46% of the programme group reached it at 1 year,2 and 36% still had it at 2 years.3 By year 5, of those in remission at year 2, 26% were still in remission.4

What experts mean by remission

An international expert group, convened by the American Diabetes Association and others, chose the word remission rather than cure. Their usual test is an HbA1c below 6.5% (48 mmol/mol), measured at least 3 months after stopping all glucose-lowering medicine.1

Why not "cure"? Because the diabetes can come back. Staying in remission needs ongoing care and regular checks. That is why we use "remission" on this site.

What the UK DiRECT trial found

DiRECT is the best-known trial. It randomised GP practices in Scotland and England to give a weight-management programme or usual care.2

TimeResult for the programme group
1 year68 of 149 people (46%) in remission2
2 years36% in remission, with an average weight loss of 7.6 kg3
5 yearsOf those in remission at year 2, 26% were still in remission. About 13% of everyone with data at 5 years were in remission.4

Two cautions. First, the programme was intense. It began with stopping diabetes and blood pressure medicine, then a formula diet of about 825 to 853 calories a day for 3 to 5 months, then slow food return and long support.2 Second, remission fell over time as weight returned. The researchers say weight regain is the problem to solve.5

What about Indians?

Indian data is newer. A 2025 study followed 2,384 Indian adults in a one-year online lifestyle programme. 31.2% reached remission by the same HbA1c definition.6 That study had no control group and may be biased by who stays in a paid programme.

Read the full breakdown in what the Indian study found.

Who is more likely to get there?

The Indian study found remission was more likely with a shorter time since diagnosis, a lower starting HbA1c, a younger age, no previous diabetes medicine, and weight loss of more than 10%.6 The DiRECT team also describes sustained weight loss as the main driver.5

This points one way: earlier is better. See just diagnosed? and young-onset diabetes.

What remission is not

  • Not a cure. Sugar can rise again, especially if weight returns. See long-term maintenance.
  • Not for everyone. In both studies, most people did not reach remission. Many still improved, which is worth having.
  • Not a reason to stop medicine yourself. In both studies, doctors managed medicine changes. See managing without medicine.
  • Not a miracle product. No supplement or "secret" is behind these results. See home remedies: do they work?

What to do with this

  1. Ask your doctor whether your type, duration and medicines make remission a realistic goal for you.
  2. Aim for steady weight loss if you carry extra weight. See weight loss with diabetes.
  3. Track your own numbers. The tracking system and our free tracker show your real trend.
  4. Plan for keeping it off. Remission fades when weight returns, so the plan must be one you can live with.

Before You Ask

Frequently asked questions

Can type 2 diabetes be reversed?

For some people it can go into remission. Experts define that as an HbA1c below 6.5% at least 3 months after stopping diabetes medicine. It is not a cure, and it can come back.

What percentage of people reach remission?

In the UK DiRECT trial, 46% of the programme group were in remission at 1 year and 36% at 2 years. In a 2025 Indian study of an online lifestyle programme, 31.2% reached remission in a year. Most people in each study did not.

Does remission last?

Often not forever. In DiRECT, of those in remission at 2 years, 26% were still in remission at 5 years. Weight regain was the main problem.

Why do experts avoid the word cure?

Because the condition can return. Experts therefore use the word remission and advise regular checks.

Who is most likely to reach remission?

People diagnosed more recently, with a lower starting HbA1c and who lose more weight, in the studies we reviewed. Earlier action works better.

Is it safe to stop my medicine if my sugar improves?

Not on your own. In the studies, doctors reduced medicine in a planned way. Stopping alone can cause problems. Talk to your doctor first.
